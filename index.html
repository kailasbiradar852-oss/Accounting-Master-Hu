Accounting learning path

Start with fundamentals, then move into reporting and business accounting.

Beginner

1. Accounting fundamentals

Understand transactions, accounts, the accounting equation, and the purpose of bookkeeping.

Open lesson

Accounting records business transactions in a structured way so owners can understand performance, assets, liabilities, and cash.

Assets = Liabilities + Owner’s Equity

Assets: what the business owns. Liabilities: what it owes. Equity: the owner’s residual interest.

Every transaction should be supported by evidence such as an invoice, receipt, bank record, or approved voucher.

Beginner

2. Double-entry bookkeeping

Learn debits, credits, journals, ledgers, and trial balances.

Open lesson

Each transaction affects at least two accounts. Total debits must equal total credits.

Debit total = Credit total

Typical balances: assets and expenses normally increase by debit; liabilities, equity, and income normally increase by credit. Contra accounts and special cases exist.

Workflow: source document → journal/voucher → ledger → trial balance → adjustments → financial statements.

Beginner

3. Financial statements

Read the profit and loss statement, balance sheet, and cash-flow statement.

Open lesson

Income statement: income less expenses over a period. Balance sheet: assets, liabilities and equity at a date. Cash-flow statement: cash from operating, investing and financing activities.

Profit = Revenue − Expenses

Profit is not the same as cash: credit sales can create profit before cash is collected, and loan repayments affect cash differently from expenses.

Business

4. Receivables & payables

Control customer balances, supplier bills, due dates, and overdue accounts.

Open lesson

Receivables are amounts customers owe; payables are amounts the business owes suppliers. Reconcile ledgers regularly against statements and receipts.

Ageing buckets often include current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90 and over 90 days. Use agreed credit terms and a consistent ageing basis.

Reconcile opening balance + invoices − credit notes − receipts = closing receivable (subject to other adjustments).

Business

5. Inventory & cost of sales

Track stock, stock valuation, gross profit, and slow-moving items.

Open lesson

Common cost-flow methods include FIFO and weighted average, subject to the applicable accounting framework. Keep item codes, units, receipts, issues, returns, and physical counts consistent.

COGS = Opening Inventory + Net Purchases + Direct Costs − Closing Inventory
Gross Profit = Net Sales − Cost of Goods Sold

Compare physical stock with system stock; investigate negative stock, unusual adjustments, obsolete items, and items with no sales in a chosen period.

Business

6. Costing & imports

Calculate landed cost from supplier price and eligible import expenses.

Open lesson

Potential landed-cost components include purchase price, freight, insurance, customs duty, inspection fees and other directly attributable costs. Recoverable VAT is generally treated differently from non-recoverable taxes; confirm local rules.

Landed cost = Purchase cost + Freight + Insurance + Duty + Other attributable costs

For foreign-currency purchases, record the transaction using the applicable exchange rate and follow the accounting rules for settlement differences. Allocate shared freight using a reasonable, consistent basis.

Core control

7. Bank reconciliation

Match the cash book with the bank statement and explain differences.

Open lesson

Start with the bank statement and cash-book balances. Identify deposits in transit, unpresented payments, bank fees, direct credits, errors, and duplicate entries.

Post missing bank charges or receipts to the books after verification. Do not use unexplained “balancing” entries to force the reconciliation to zero.

Core accounting

8. Fixed assets & depreciation

Record equipment, vehicles, buildings, useful lives, and disposals.

Open lesson

Capitalise qualifying asset costs under the applicable accounting policy. Keep an asset register with cost, acquisition date, location, useful life, residual value, accumulated depreciation and disposal details.

Straight-line depreciation per year = (Cost − Residual value) ÷ Useful life

Accounting depreciation is not automatically the same as tax capital allowances. Check local tax rules separately.

Analysis

9. Budgeting & ratios

Use budgets and ratios to spot cash pressure and performance changes.

Open lesson
Current ratio = Current assets ÷ Current liabilities
Gross margin % = Gross profit ÷ Net sales × 100
Net margin % = Net profit ÷ Net sales × 100
Working capital = Current assets − Current liabilities

Compare ratios over time and with relevant industry context. A single ratio rarely tells the whole story.

TallyPrime practical reference

Voucher types

Sales, Purchase, Receipt, Payment, Contra, Journal, Debit Note and Credit Note record different transaction types. Choose the voucher that matches the substance of the transaction.

Ledgers & groups

Create ledgers under appropriate groups: customers under Sundry Debtors, suppliers under Sundry Creditors, bank accounts under Bank Accounts, and expense/income ledgers under their relevant groups.

Inventory setup

Use consistent stock item names, groups, units of measure, godowns, and opening balances. Avoid creating duplicate stock items with slightly different spellings.

Outstanding bills

Enable bill-wise details where appropriate, enter due dates and credit periods correctly, and reconcile party ledgers. Review overdue bills regularly.

Tax & invoice printing

Check company tax settings, party registrations, item tax classifications, and invoice configuration. Test print changes on a copy or backup before applying custom TDL.

Backup & controls

Back up company data before changes, restrict user permissions, keep audit trails where available, and test restore procedures. Do not install unknown TDL files on live company data.

Menu names and tax features can vary by TallyPrime release and company configuration. For version-specific changes, confirm the exact release and test in a backup company first.

Accounting glossary

Accrual basis

Recognise income when earned and expenses when incurred, where the applicable accounting rules require it, rather than simply when cash moves.

Accounts receivable

Amounts customers owe the business for goods or services already supplied.

Accounts payable

Amounts the business owes suppliers and other creditors.

Provision

A liability of uncertain timing or amount, recognised only when the relevant accounting criteria are met.

Reconciliation

Comparing two sets of records and explaining or correcting differences with evidence.

Materiality

Whether an omission or misstatement could reasonably influence users’ decisions, judged in context.

Working capital

Current assets minus current liabilities; one indicator of short-term financial flexibility.

Trial balance

A list of ledger account balances used to check whether total debits equal total credits. It does not prove every entry is correct.

Withholding tax

Tax withheld from certain payments and remitted to the tax authority when required by local law.
